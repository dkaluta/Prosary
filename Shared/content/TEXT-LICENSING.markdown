# Original text and source licensing

All original natural-language text authored by David Kaluta and included in Prosary
is dedicated under **CC0 1.0 Universal (CC0-1.0)**. The operative dedication is in the
canonical [root LICENSE](../../LICENSE), alongside the BSD 2-Clause license for
software and other original material outside this dedication. See the
[CC0 deed](https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/) and
[legal code](https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/legalcode).

The dedication covers his prose, prayers, translations, documentation, interface wording,
and editorial contributions, to the extent he holds copyright or related rights in
them. It includes adaptations of public-domain texts, transcription corrections,
verse and word segmentation, disclosed restorations, and editorial notes. It follows
his text into canonical data, generated prayer packs, Bible archives, native copies,
web pages, and source files. Original natural-language wording embedded in a source
file follows CC0; software code, processing tools, and nontextual assets retain their
separate applicable licenses.

Source credit and rights remain separate. Sourced third-party text retains its original
terms; the dedication covers only David Kaluta's own rights and contributions, including
his contributions to adaptations or translations. Prosary retains translator and edition
credits, scan references, and review evidence, and distinguishes editorial restorations
from legible source text. CC0 imposes no attribution condition on David Kaluta's dedicated
contributions; existing third-party requirements still apply to their own material.

The Hebrew prayers sourced from the St James Vicariate, the Hebrew Mission prayers,
and the sourced prayer “O My Jesus” are specifically outside this dedication. Their
prayer texts retain their existing source credits and applicable terms; inclusion in
Prosary does not make them David Kaluta's original writing or relicense them under CC0.
His separately authored notes about those prayers may fall within the dedication,
without changing the rights in the prayers themselves.

This policy does not declare a whole source collection, a scan, or a modern translation
to be public domain. Each underlying work and edition requires its own source and
rights assessment. In particular, the Kahana collection contains contributions by
different translators; this dedication does not settle their respective rights or
remove any existing source-review restriction.
