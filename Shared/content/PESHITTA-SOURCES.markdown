# Peshitta Scripture sources

The Aramaic Scripture in the Rosary, Stations of the Cross, Via Lucis, and O Antiphons is generated by `Shared/tools/import-scripture.py`. Run it with `--language arc`; do not edit the generated Scripture fields by hand. The supplied non-Scripture prayers keep their own source wording.

## New Testament

The 49 New Testament passages use the **Digital Syriac Corpus** edition of *The New Testament in Syriac* (London: British and Foreign Bible Society, 1905), edited by G. H. Gwilliam, J. Pinkerton, and John Gwynn. George A. Kiraz transcribed the Syriac; James E. Walters encoded the TEI XML edition.

The source headers declare the Syriac base text public domain and the electronic edition **[CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/)**. This credit applies to the actual pointed electronic edition used here. It does not rely on the different terms of SEDRA III or of another unpointed transcription.

- [Matthew](https://syriaccorpus.org/100)
- [Mark](https://syriaccorpus.org/119)
- [Luke](https://syriaccorpus.org/120)
- [John](https://syriaccorpus.org/121)
- [Acts](https://syriaccorpus.org/122)
- [Revelation](https://syriaccorpus.org/145)
- [Source files pinned to revision 833adc148cc356a6c70c16f81b22df9188df717a](https://github.com/srophe/syriac-corpus/tree/833adc148cc356a6c70c16f81b22df9188df717a/data/tei)

Prosary extracts only numbered verses from the requested chapters and normalizes XML layout whitespace. The source's letters, Western Syriac vowel signs, and punctuation remain in the Syriac reading track. Erez's supplied converter produces the Hebrew-script track, including contextual final letters and the vowel signs his rules support. Unsupported marks are omitted only from that projection. No vocalization is guessed from an unpointed word.

The small Luke 1:26–28 regression fixture retains the source's attribution and license header. The importer rejects missing vowel signs, wrong book identities, duplicate verse numbers in a requested chapter, and unhandled editorial notes. The pinned source has duplicate verse numbering in Luke 11, which is not used by these devotions; attempting to import that chapter fails explicitly.

## Old Testament

The seven Isaiah passages use nine pointed verses from the [user-supplied Internet Archive XML](https://archive.org/details/peshitta-complete-bible-otnt). On 2026-09-07 the user reported that Erez appeared to approve these verses and requested their integration. This reports the user's review context; it does not identify or independently verify the underlying edition or its redistribution terms, which remain unresolved. The BFBS 1905 attribution and CC BY 4.0 license of the separate New Testament edition above do **not** apply to this Isaiah source.

The importer downloads and caches the supplied complete XML only when its SHA-256 is `4f71fe418a1d23f6b65d63d155f838a228dbdb6e4857834f4503afdcf009ea88`. Every cache read is checked again. Extraction is restricted to Isaiah 7:14; 9:2; 11:2–5, 10; 22:22; and 28:16. These nine verses, with source notes, also form a scoped offline regression fixture. No other Old Testament material is imported from this file.

The original Syriac wording, vowels, and punctuation are retained, with XML layout whitespace normalized; Erez's converter produces the paired Hebrew-script text. This source numbers the darkness/light verse **9:2**, replacing the earlier ETCBC edition's 9:1 citation. Its Isaiah 11:2 has `ܕܝܕܥܬܐ`, and Isaiah 28:16 omits the earlier text's `ܗܟܢܐ`. The importer retains these supplied readings rather than placing their vowels over the earlier consonants.

The [Isaiah source review](PESHITTA-ISAIAH-SOURCE-REVIEW.markdown) records the supplied file, metadata investigation, accepted differences, and the separately researched public-domain Walton print. The superseded ETCBC transcription had its own [CC BY-NC text terms](https://github.com/ETCBC/peshitta/blob/master/docs/about.md), distinct from that repository's MIT tooling license; neither license is assigned to the replacement text.
